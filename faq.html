FAQ

Questions, answered.

Grounded in how AutoDoc actually works — on-device AI, local storage, optional calendars, and opt-in analytics.

AutoDoc is a free, open-source meeting notes app for macOS and Windows, built by Duet Display. It records screen video, microphone, and system audio, then transcribes the audio and creates editable notes locally. You can replay recording moments from timestamps, search your meetings, and ask questions with Ask AI.
AutoDoc runs transcription, notes, and Ask AI locally and keeps replayable screen recordings on your computer. That requires local storage, model downloads, and processing resources. Other products make different tradeoffs; see our sourced Granola comparison for a specific example.
Search finds matching text in transcripts and notes, then opens the meeting so you can review the context. Ask AI answers questions from your meeting history. Retrieval and responses run locally through Ollama. Review the answers before you rely on them.
No. AutoDoc captures screen video, system audio, and your microphone from your device — the same way local screen recording works. No participant named "AutoDoc" joins your call, and you don't need host permission for a third-party bot.
AutoDoc has been tested with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack, Discord, and browser-based calls (Safari, Chrome, Edge, Arc, and others). Because it captures system audio directly, it should work with most meeting applications without platform-specific integrations.
Windows uses Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 and automatically selects DirectML GPU acceleration or a CPU-optimized model. Apple Silicon Macs use MLX Whisper with distil-large-v3. Notes and Ask AI use a dedicated local Ollama instance that AutoDoc manages separately from any Ollama you may have installed. No meeting content is sent to the cloud for AI inference.
Recording, transcription, and notes work offline after setup. Initial setup downloads local models and runtimes. App and model updates, optional Google or Microsoft calendar connections, and opt-in diagnostics also use the network. See our privacy details.
AutoDoc creates a summary and conversation topics with editable details. Available timestamps let you check the corresponding moment in the recording. Review generated notes before relying on them.
Updating does not automatically regenerate your existing meeting notes. You can reprocess a meeting when you want to generate new notes. Older notes remain supported.

AutoDoc labels microphone audio as Me and combined system audio as Them. It does not identify individual remote participants. Manual label changes and calendar name suggestions do not separate voices within the system-audio track.
Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook. Calendar access is optional — used to show upcoming meetings, name recordings, suggest speaker names from attendees, and set auto-record modes (off, once, or series). OAuth tokens are stored encrypted on your device.
Meeting data lives under ~/Library/Application Support/AutoDoc/ on macOS and %APPDATA%\AutoDoc\ on Windows — including recordings, transcripts, notes, and models. Meeting data is encrypted at rest. AutoDoc does not operate servers that store your meeting content.
AutoDoc encrypts stored meeting data with AES-256-GCM. When operating-system credential storage is available, macOS Keychain or Windows DPAPI protects the encryption key. Otherwise, the key is stored locally without operating-system protection. Files you export are separate from AutoDoc's encrypted storage.

8 GB RAM is supported. 16 GB+ is recommended for the best experience on both platforms. Plan for ~10 GB free for first-run model downloads. Intel Macs and Windows on ARM are not supported.

Minimum (supported) Recommended (best experience)
macOS macOS 14+ · Apple Silicon (M1+) · 8 GB RAM 16 GB+ Apple Silicon
Windows Windows 10+ · 64-bit x64 · 8 GB RAM 16 GB+ RAM · 8+ logical processors · optional DirectML GPU with 4 GB+ VRAM

On 8 GB machines, AutoDoc uses a smaller local notes model and a lower-impact path: audio is processed sequentially and notes wait until transcription finishes. Expect a longer wait after the meeting. A GPU on Windows can speed up transcription, but it does not replace the 16 GB+ RAM recommendation.
